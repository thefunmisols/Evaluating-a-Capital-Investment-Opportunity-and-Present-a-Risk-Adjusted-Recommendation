# Capital Investment Analysis & Risk-Adjusted Recommendation

## Executive Summary

Capital allocation is one of the most important responsibilities of a finance professional.

A business should not invest in an opportunity simply because it generates the **highest revenue**. Management must understand the expected profitability, downside exposure, economic environment, operational implications, and whether the investment supports the company's strategic priorities.

In this project, I evaluated **two competing capital investment opportunities — Option A and Option B — and developed a risk-adjusted recommendation for the CFO.**

The analysis combined **financial performance, risk assessment, macroeconomic considerations, KPI impact, and executive-level decision making** to determine which investment offered the stronger overall business case.

---

## Project Objective

The objective was to answer a key capital allocation question:

> **Which investment opportunity should the company pursue when both financial returns and business risks are considered?**

Rather than focusing solely on revenue, I evaluated each option across:

* Revenue potential
* Operating profitability
* Operating margin
* Capital requirements
* Financial risk
* Market risk
* Operational risk
* Inflation exposure
* Interest-rate sensitivity
* Value at Risk (VaR)
* Strategic KPI alignment
* Long-term sustainability

The final recommendation was designed to provide management with a clear basis for allocating capital.

# Step 3 — Risk Matrix

A risk matrix is a tool that is normally used to assess the level of risk and assist the decision-making process. Financial returns tell only part of the investment story. 

I therefore developed a qualitative risk matrix covering three major risk categories:

### Financial Risk

Assessed potential exposure relating to capital requirements, financing costs, profitability uncertainty, cash-flow pressure, and downside financial outcomes.

### Market Risk

Evaluated how changes in customer demand, market conditions, competition, inflation, and broader economic conditions could affect expected returns.

### Operational Risk

Considered the complexity of implementing and operating each investment, including execution challenges, resource requirements, process dependencies, and potential disruptions.

Each investment was classified as:

**🟢 Low Risk | 🟡 Moderate Risk | 🔴 High Risk**

# Value at Risk & Downside Thinking

An important part of the analysis was considering **Value at Risk (VaR)** as a framework for understanding potential downside exposure.

Instead of asking only:

> *How much could the company earn?*

I also considered:

> **How much value could the business potentially lose if assumptions or economic conditions move against the investment?**

This risk-adjusted perspective is particularly important when management is allocating significant capital under uncertainty.

# Step 5 — KPI & Economic Signal Analysis

Capital budgeting does not happen in isolation from the economy.

I therefore assessed how changes in **interest rates and inflation** could affect both investment options.

<img width="957" height="480" alt="KPI and Economic Analysis" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/0a55c6a0-75bf-4fe9-a0e0-3d97e9fc750c" />

### Rising Interest Rates

Higher interest rates can:

* Increase the cost of debt financing
* Increase the company's required rate of return
* Reduce the present value of future cash flows
* Make highly leveraged investments less attractive
* Put additional pressure on profitability and cash flow

### Inflation

Inflation can increase:

* Labour costs
* Supplier costs
* Operating expenditure
* Equipment and replacement costs
* Working-capital requirements

An investment that appears profitable under current assumptions may therefore become less attractive when inflation-adjusted costs are incorporated.

# Key Lessons From the Project

One of my biggest takeaways from this project is that:

> **The investment with the highest revenue is not automatically the best investment.**

Good capital allocation requires management to understand the relationship between:

**Return + Risk + Cash Flow + Economic Conditions + Strategic Alignment**

A strong finance professional must therefore move beyond reporting historical numbers and help management answer forward-looking questions such as:

* What return can we reasonably expect?
* What assumptions drive that return?
* What happens if those assumptions fail?
* How much capital are we putting at risk?
* Can the company absorb the downside?
* Which KPIs will improve or deteriorate?
* Does the investment support our long-term strategy?
* Is there a better use of the company's capital?

These are the questions that transform financial analysis into **strategic financial decision support**.
